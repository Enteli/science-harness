# Results: what the existing experiments establish

These are aggregate results from the completed, registered DiscoverPhysics
studies summarized in the seminar deck. They are **not measurements from a new
package release**, an official-paper reproduction, or a claim that external
notebook memory dominates full history.
Historical source/protocol epochs and frozen private evaluation records remain
separate from this reusable source distribution.

## Completed main study

GPT-6.1 Sol/high; Yukawa and Oscillator; five paired starts per world; ten
investigations per arm. Main-study source: `8436220d`.

| Arm | Ever reached target | Passing submitted final | Mean first-hit experiments |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Legacy B0 | 9/10 | 7/10 | Not reported here: two native censors were excluded from its original mean |
| Full history | 10/10 | 9/10 | 8.6 |
| Full history + review | 9/10 | 9/10 | 11.4 |
| External notebook | 9/10 | 7/10 | 13.8 |
| External notebook + review | 10/10 | 8/10 | 8.1 |

Across all five arms, **47/50 ever reached the predictive target; 40/50 retained
a passing final**. There were 46 normal completions, two external-notebook response-protocol
failures and two legacy native zero-separation censors. Censors are not ordinary
physics failures. Four separately selected recovery diagnostics are not
substituted into these headline counts.

A predictive pass uses registered position MSE divided by the declared scale
squared, at most 0.01, over 32 clean held-out launches and five times.
First hit is retrospective, invisible to the scientist, and includes the two
startup experiments. For the reported memory-policy means, never passing costs 51.
Submitted-final accuracy is separate: a historical hit does not certify the final.

## Active versus random controls

The same scientist still analyzed evidence and fitted laws in random arms;
only executed experiment selection changed.

| Memory | Historical active first-hit cost | Random first-hit cost | Passing finals: active / random |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full history | 8.6 | 26.0 | 9/10 vs 4/10 |
| External notebook | 13.8 | 27.4 | 7/10 vs 3/10 |

Random controls reached the target at some point in 17/20 runs. Among the 16
pairs where both arms passed, active was earlier in 15. This is **descriptive,
not a causal selector-effect estimate**: historical active runs used 100-call
caps; random controls mixed preserved 100-call and amended 500-call epochs.
Source changes and remote-service drift are additional confounds.

## Why make external notebook the default interface?

External notebook memory makes persistent scientific memory and retrieval explicit and inspectable.
It is **not** the empirical winner on every metric. In this main study, mean
scientist-call input was 11,917 tokens for external notebook versus 17,414 for
full history, but notebook lookups brought total input to 2.6 times full history's
and credits to 4.08 times full history's.
External notebook + review improved this particular first-hit average while using more inference;
that does not isolate a pure critique effect or guarantee improvement elsewhere.

Historical records use the original labels: B = full history; C = external
notebook; B0 = the legacy baseline. Display names changed, not the frozen data,
protocol or outcomes.

The three-species result of a pass at 30 experiments is a separate single-case
pilot, not a replicated agency advantage. Incomplete/operationally stopped
three-species memory/review cohorts and local unqualified open-model diagnostics
are not promoted into release benchmarks.

## Provenance and reproduction limits

Separate from the historical studies above, two bounded Opus 5.5 integration
attempts now have [recorded outcomes](crossbench-results.md): one NewtonBench
energy-law recovery and one controlled MADE analytic optimization. Both passed
their custom numerical criteria. These single-case demonstrations do not
extend the historical comparison or establish a memory-policy effect.

The summary follows the seminar's completed main-study, random-control and
per-call context evidence, not screenshots or reconstruction of missing private
panels. Original run traces, billing authorizations, private arrays and SDK
session logs are intentionally not distributed. The registry/config/source
hashes support new explicitly registered studies; current models and newly
generated panels cannot byte-reproduce historical private outcomes.

For new comparisons, freeze the protocol, model/runtime, noise, normalization,
policy and budgets; retain assigned and censored cases; distinguish first hits
from accepted finals; report uncertainty and source/operational epochs. No
intermediate or final private result may coach an unfinished scientist.
